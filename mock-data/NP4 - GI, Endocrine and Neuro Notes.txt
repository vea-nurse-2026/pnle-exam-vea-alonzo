NP IV NUTRITION, GI, METABOLISM, ENDOCRINE, PERCEPTION AND COORDINATION - REVIEW NOTES (SAMPLE)

Nutrition and Diet Therapy

To prevent dumping syndrome after gastric surgery, the client eats small, frequent meals that are high in protein and low in carbohydrates, drinks fluids between meals rather than with meals, and should lie down for 20 to 30 minutes after meals.

In celiac disease, avoid gluten found in barley, rye, oats, and wheat.

Gastrointestinal

Appendicitis causes pain at McBurney's point and rebound tenderness. Never apply heat to the abdomen and do not give laxatives or enemas, because these may cause the appendix to rupture.

Pain from a duodenal ulcer is often relieved by food, while pain from a gastric ulcer often worsens with food.

Metabolism and Endocrine

Diabetic ketoacidosis presents with hyperglycemia, ketones, Kussmaul respirations, and a fruity breath odor.

Regular insulin is the only insulin that can be given intravenously.

For hypoglycemia in a conscious client, give 15 grams of fast-acting carbohydrate and recheck blood glucose in 15 minutes.

Perception and Coordination

A change in level of consciousness is the earliest sign of increased intracranial pressure. Late signs are bradycardia, widening pulse pressure, and irregular respirations. Elevate the head of the bed to 30 degrees.

The earliest sign of compartment syndrome is pain unrelieved by analgesics and increased by passive stretching. Assess the 5 Ps: pain, pallor, pulselessness, paresthesia, and paralysis.

Clients with glaucoma receive miotics such as pilocarpine; atropine is avoided.
