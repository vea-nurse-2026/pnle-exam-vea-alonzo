# NP V Maladaptive Behavior, Life-Threatening and Emergency Care - Review Notes (SAMPLE)

## Therapeutic Communication

Avoid asking 'why' questions, which put clients on the defensive. Use open-ended questions, reflection, clarification, and therapeutic silence.

During a panic attack, stay with the client, keep the environment calm, and give short, simple directions.

## Mood Disorders

Suicide risk may increase when energy improves after antidepressant therapy begins, often within 10 to 14 days. Ask clients directly about suicidal thoughts.

The therapeutic lithium level is 0.6 to 1.2 mEq/L. Clients should keep a normal sodium intake and drink 2 to 3 liters of fluid daily.

Clients taking MAO inhibitors must avoid foods high in tyramine, such as aged cheese, red wine, and cured meats, to prevent a hypertensive crisis.

## Schizophrenia and Psychosis

Positive symptoms include hallucinations and delusions; negative symptoms include flat affect, alogia, and avolition.

Neuroleptic malignant syndrome presents with high fever, muscle rigidity, and altered consciousness. Stop the antipsychotic and notify the physician immediately.

## Substance Use

Alcohol withdrawal symptoms begin 6 to 24 hours after the last drink; delirium tremens may appear 48 to 72 hours later. Thiamine is given to prevent Wernicke's encephalopathy.

## Emergency and High-Acuity Care

Rule of nines for adults: head 9%, each arm 9%, each leg 18%, anterior trunk 18%, posterior trunk 18%, and perineum 1%.

Parkland formula: 4 mL x body weight in kg x percent of total body surface area burned, over 24 hours; half of the total is given in the first 8 hours.

Adult CPR: compress the chest at a rate of 100 to 120 per minute and a depth of at least 5 cm, with a ratio of 30 compressions to 2 breaths.
